# Vesper Standard Time

What if clocks moved a few seconds every night, so we never had to change them again? Vesper Standard Time (VST) is a proposed alternative to Daylight Saving Time — instead of two jarring 60-minute jumps a year, it makes imperceptible nightly adjustments (about 20 seconds on average) to keep sunset near the same time year-round.

**Live:** [vesperstandardtime.com](https://vesperstandardtime.com/)

> "Your body loves routine. VST finally gives it one."

## The problem with DST

Twice a year, everyone's sleep cycle gets disrupted by a sudden 60-minute shift — a change linked to increased risk of heart attacks and accidents in the days after. It's also a jarring misalignment forced onto a natural, gradual solar cycle.

## How VST works

Instead of one big jump, VST spreads the same seasonal adjustment across every night of the year in tiny, imperceptible increments — averaging about 20 seconds a night. Sunset stays close to the same clock time year-round instead of swinging by hours between summer and winter.

## Why "Vesper"

Vesper refers to the evening star (Venus) — the name reflects what the system stabilizes: the evening boundary, not the amount of daylight itself. VST isn't about adding more light; it's about making evenings get dark, and mornings get light, at a predictable, consistent pace.

## The interface

The live site shows three time modes side by side:

- **VST** — the adjusted Vesper time
- **Standard** — regular clock time for reference
- **Split** — the current drift between the two, in seconds
